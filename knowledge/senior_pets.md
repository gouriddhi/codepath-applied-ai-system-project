# Senior Pet Care Guidelines

Dogs are generally considered senior from about age 7–8 (large breeds earlier, around 6), and cats from about age 10–11.

## Exercise
- Keep exercise daily but gentler: replace one long walk with two or three shorter walks of 15–20 minutes.
- For senior cats, offer shorter, low-intensity play sessions (5–10 minutes) two or three times a day.
- Watch for stiffness after activity and shorten sessions if it appears.

## Feeding
- Senior pets often need fewer calories but more easily digestible protein; ask a vet about a senior diet.
- Smaller, more frequent meals (2–3 per day) can help with digestion and appetite.
- Weigh your pet monthly; unexplained weight loss or gain should be reported to a vet.
- Make water easy to reach on every floor of the home.

## Comfort and grooming
- Provide soft, supportive bedding away from drafts.
- Brush several times a week (5–10 minutes); older pets groom themselves less well.
- Check nails every 2 weeks; less activity means nails wear down more slowly.
- Check teeth and gums weekly for redness, odor, or broken teeth.

## Health monitoring
- Vet checkups every 6 months, including bloodwork.
- Keep a simple daily note of appetite, water intake, and energy level.

## Warning signs — contact a vet
- Increased thirst or urination.
- Confusion, getting stuck in corners, or changes in sleep pattern.
- New lumps, bad breath, or difficulty eating.
- Reluctance to climb stairs or jump.
