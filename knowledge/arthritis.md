# Arthritis Care Guidelines

Arthritis is common in senior pets and in large or overweight dogs, but it can occur at any age.

## Exercise
- Gentle, consistent daily exercise is better than occasional long sessions; it keeps joints moving and muscles strong.
- For dogs, aim for two or three short, slow walks of 10–20 minutes on soft, level ground instead of one long walk.
- Avoid jumping, stairs, rough play, and running on hard surfaces.
- Spend 5 minutes warming up with slow walking before more activity.
- For cats, encourage light play (5 minutes, a few times a day) and provide ramps or steps to favorite spots.

## Weight and feeding
- Keeping a lean body weight is one of the most effective ways to reduce joint pain.
- Ask a vet about joint-support diets or supplements such as omega-3 fatty acids.

## Medication
- Pain medication (often NSAIDs) is usually given once or twice daily with food; follow vet dosing exactly.
- Never substitute human pain relievers.

## Home comfort
- Provide thick orthopedic bedding in a warm spot.
- Add rugs or non-slip mats on slippery floors.
- Use a low-sided litter box for cats.
- Gentle massage or range-of-motion exercises (5–10 minutes daily) can help, if a vet approves.

## Grooming
- Arthritic pets may struggle to groom hips and back; brush these areas several times a week.
- Keep nails short so the pet walks with good posture.

## Warning signs — contact a vet
- Limping that gets worse or appears suddenly.
- Yelping or aggression when touched.
- Refusing to walk, stand, or use the litter box.
- Vomiting or dark stool while on pain medication.
