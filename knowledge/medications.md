# Medication Guidelines

## Timing
- Give medications at the same time every day; set the schedule around existing meal times so doses aren't forgotten.
- "Twice daily" usually means every 12 hours (for example 8 am and 8 pm), not just morning and lunch.
- Some medications must be given with food (many anti-inflammatories), others on an empty stomach; follow the label or vet instructions.
- Monthly preventives (flea, tick, heartworm) should be given on the same date each month.
- Giving a pill usually takes about 5 minutes; allow extra time for pets that resist.

## Giving medication safely
- Hide pills in a small amount of soft food or a pill pocket, or place at the back of the tongue and gently hold the mouth closed.
- Follow a pill with a small treat or water so it doesn't stick in the throat (especially important for cats).
- Never split, crush, or skip doses without vet approval.
- Never give human medications; common ones like ibuprofen, acetaminophen, and naproxen can be fatal to pets.

## Missed doses
- If a dose is missed, give it when remembered unless it is almost time for the next one; never double up.
- Keep a simple log of each dose given, especially when more than one person cares for the pet.

## Storage
- Store medications out of reach of pets and children, at the temperature on the label.

## Warning signs — contact a vet
- Vomiting, diarrhea, or loss of appetite after starting a new medication.
- Black or tarry stool (possible stomach bleeding from anti-inflammatories).
- Unusual sleepiness, agitation, or facial swelling.
